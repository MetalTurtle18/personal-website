# Contents

# Introduction

Dungeons and Dragons usually requires maps on the table. The game master frequently needs to get up and manipulate the map. It can be annoying to move the map when there are things on top, and it can sometimes be impossible to move it without disrupting the state of game characters sitting on the map.

My solution is to put a TV horizontally in a tabletop. My table lets a game master control what is displayed on the screen through a laptop. Then player miniatures and other game pieces go on top of the TV screen. This way, the game master can move and manipulate the map endlessly without disturbing the pieces on top.

The table must disassemble small enough to be transported in a car. This means the table top is designed to break into four sections and that the legs are removable.

## Second to Last design pivot

Then I talked to Mike about ideas for the rest of the table. He suggested bolts and wing nuts to attach the quadrants together. I decided that it would be best to do away with the hinges altogether and just attach the four pieces with bolts.

I clamped the pieces together and drilled holes for bolts and wing nuts. I had to use this right angle drill bit to drill holes. Some of the sections were too close together to fit the whole drill with the bit. I had to use a smaller bit that fit in the right angle adapter and then take the pieces apart and re drill them with a bigger bit.

Since the wood was so warped, it took some force to screw the pieces together after drilling. I took to whacking them together with a mallet while trying to tighten the wing nuts. This worked well enough.

This also let me flip the assembled top up onto a table and put the TV in to simulate how it would look finished.

## Last design pivot

At this point I also rethought my leg idea. I am no longer going to do the secondary table for support. I will stick with the four legs as I initially intended. I think this way it will look better, I already have the wood for it, and it will be easier and faster to create before the design fair. I printed the main body parts for all the final TV supports with high infill and 8 perimeters. They are really strong even though they are just PLA. They have two nuts and the bolt spins within it. The bolts were a bit hard to turn on their own, so I designed these knobs to go on them to make them easier to turn. I ended up printing them out of nylon since it was already loaded into the MK2.5. They came out well! Then I mounted all of them onto the table pieces.

## Opensauce 2024

The year after I finished this project and graduated high school, I brought it to [OpenSauce](https://www.opensauce.com) 2024, which is essentially the reincarnation of Maker Faire Bay Area with a focus on creators and panels (and hopefully financially viable long-term). I was an exhibitor and had to request a double booth to accomodate my enourmous table. It was quite an ordeal to pack the table, legs, TV, and all the other stuff I needed to exhibit into my car, but it was doable with some help.

I had a great time at this event and went back in 2026, though not with the table, which is not really in a workable state at the moment. I would absolutely recommend it, even though it is a bit pricey for non-exhibitors. Below are some photos I took at the event that show off my project, some 35mm scans from photos I took on my Nikon FG, and some photos of YouTube creators whose videos I enjoy watching who came to see my exhibit.
